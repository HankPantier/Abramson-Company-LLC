## Get a clear picture of your retirement readiness

Scattered 401(k) balances from old jobs. A SEP IRA you opened years ago and haven't looked at since. A vague sense that retirement is "handled," but no real proof of it. That's the situation many business owners and professionals in Westport bring to us, and it's exactly what a retirement plan analysis is built to fix.

A CPA-led review pulls every account into one picture: what you have, what it's costing you in taxes, and whether it lines up with the retirement you actually want. Abramson & Company has guided Fairfield County clients through this process since founding the firm in 2005, using the same plain-spoken approach behind our [tax and business advisory work](/services/tax). No jargon, no sales pitch, just a clear read on where you stand.

Retirement planning looks different depending on how you earn your income. Here's who tends to gain the most from a formal review.

icon: Scale

We flag where fees, redundancy, or missed contribution room are quietly costing you. [Professional service providers](/industries/professional-service-providers) we work with often pair this with ongoing tax strategy.

### Video Production Companies
icon: Video

Production businesses deal with project-based income that swings from year to year, making consistent retirement contributions a challenge. A CPA-led review maps contribution options against your actual cash flow, so you're building toward retirement even in leaner production cycles.

### Retail Businesses
icon: ShoppingBag

Retail owners juggle seasonal revenue, payroll for hourly staff, and ongoing inventory costs, which often means retirement planning gets deferred. A review identifies how much you can set aside tax-free within your current structure and whether your plan accommodates your employees' needs.

## What our retirement plan analysis covers

A retirement plan analysis starts with the accounts you already have, not a blank slate. We gather statements from your 401(k), SEP IRA, Solo 401(k), or pension plan and evaluate them against your income, tax bracket, and timeline.

The review typically includes:

- A full inventory of existing retirement accounts and their current allocations
- An assessment of how much you're contributing versus how much you're allowed to contribute tax-free
- A comparison of your current plan structure against other tax-advantaged options
- A look at how retirement contributions interact with your [business entity type](/services/entity-type-analysis) and overall tax strategy
- Alignment between your retirement plan and any estate planning documents already in place
- A written summary of findings and recommended next steps, in language you can actually use

Nothing here is generic. Recommendations come from someone who already knows your tax return, your business structure, and your goals, because the same CPA team handles all three.

## Retirement plan options for business owners and the self-employed

Choosing between a SEP IRA and a Solo 401(k) isn't a coin flip. It depends on your income level, whether you have employees, and how aggressively you want to contribute in a given year.

A SEP IRA is simple to set up and works well for business owners with fluctuating income or a small number of employees. A Solo 401(k) often allows higher total contributions and adds a Roth option that a SEP IRA doesn't offer. For business owners with staff beyond themselves, a SIMPLE IRA or small 401(k) plan may fit better, something we often map out alongside [business foundation services](/services/business-foundation-services/).

Abramson & Company walks through the math with you, comparing contribution limits, administrative cost, and tax impact side by side, so the plan you choose fits your business today, not a generic recommendation pulled from a brochure.

**Q: What is included in a retirement plan analysis?**
A: A retirement plan analysis reviews all existing accounts, including 401(k), SEP IRA, and Solo 401(k) plans, checks contribution levels against tax-advantaged limits, and compares your current structure to other options. **Q: Should I choose a SEP IRA or a Solo 401(k) as a business owner?**
A: It depends on your income, whether you have employees, and how much you want to contribute annually. A Solo 401(k) often allows higher contributions and a Roth option; a SEP IRA is simpler to administer. A CPA review compares both against your actual numbers.
