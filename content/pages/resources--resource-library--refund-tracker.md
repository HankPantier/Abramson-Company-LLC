## Check your refund status in minutes

You don't need to dig through government websites to find out where your money is. For your federal refund, the IRS **Where's My Refund** tool at [irs.gov/refunds](https://www.irs.gov/refunds) gives you a status update within 24 hours of e-filing (or about four weeks after mailing a paper return). For your Connecticut state refund, the Department of Revenue Services runs its tracker through **myconneCT** at [portal.ct.gov/drs](https://portal.ct.gov/drs/individuals/individual-tax-page/status-of-refund). Both tools are free, and both update once a day, usually overnight, so checking more than once every 24 hours won't get you a different answer.

If you filed with Abramson & Company, we can also pull up your filing confirmation and refund amount on request, no login required on your end.

## What to have ready before you check

Both the IRS and Connecticut systems will reject your lookup if any detail is slightly off. Have these on hand before you click through:

- Your Social Security number or ITIN
- Your exact filing status (single, married filing jointly, head of household, etc.)
- The exact refund amount shown on your return, down to the dollar
- For CT specifically, the tax year you're checking
- A few extra minutes, since typing errors are the most common reason people see an error message

If you filed jointly, use the SSN listed first on the return. That trips up more people than anything else.

## Typical refund timelines

Timing depends on how you filed and how you're getting paid. E-filed federal returns with direct deposit typically arrive within 21 days. Paper federal returns run six to eight weeks, sometimes longer during peak season. Connecticut refunds generally take longer than federal ones: the DRS estimates 10 to 12 weeks for a processed return, and paper CT returns can stretch past that.

A few things speed things up: filing early, e-filing instead of mailing, and choosing direct deposit over a paper check. A few things slow things down: filing close to the April deadline, claiming certain credits that trigger extra review, or mailing an amended return, which the IRS says can take up to 16 weeks to process.

None of these timelines are promises. They're patterns we see year after year with clients across Westport, Fairfield County, and the tri-state area.

## Common reasons your refund is delayed

A delay usually isn't a red flag. It's often one of these:

A math error or a mismatched Social Security number triggers a manual review, which adds time but rarely changes the outcome. Identity verification requests, where the IRS asks you to confirm you filed the return, are increasingly common and simply require a response, not a correction. Amended returns take longer by design, since they're reviewed by hand rather than run through automated processing. And if you owe back taxes, child support, or certain federal debts, the Treasury Offset Program can reduce or redirect your refund before it reaches you, something you'd typically be notified about separately.

If your status shows "still processing" for more than 21 days past your e-file date, that's the point worth paying attention to, not panicking over.

## When to reach out to our team

Checking a portal once a day is reasonable. Refreshing it five times before lunch isn't going to change the outcome, and it's a good sign it's time to call someone who can actually look into it.

Reach out to us if your refund is more than 21 days late for an e-filed federal return, more than 12 weeks late for a Connecticut return, if you've received an identity verification letter and aren't sure how to respond, or if your refund amount doesn't match what we filed. Because our team works together on every client file, whoever picks up the phone can pull up your return and give you a straight answer, not a generic script.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Refund tracker

**Q: How long does it take to get a Connecticut tax refund?**
A: Connecticut refunds typically take 10 to 12 weeks to process through myconneCT, longer than the federal timeline. Paper returns and amended filings take additional time. If your Connecticut refund is more than 12 weeks out with no update, it's worth contacting the DRS or your CPA.

**Q: How do I check my federal tax refund status?**
A: Visit the IRS Where's My Refund tool at irs.gov/refunds and enter your Social Security number, filing status, and exact refund amount. Status updates once every 24 hours, usually overnight, so checking more frequently won't produce new information.

**Q: Why is my tax refund taking longer than expected?**
A: Common causes include math errors, identity verification requests, amended returns, or offsets for back taxes or child support. Most delays resolve without changing your refund amount. If it's been more than 21 days for an e-filed federal return, it's reasonable to look into it further.

**Q: When should I contact a CPA about a delayed refund?**
A: Reach out if your federal refund passes 21 days, your Connecticut refund passes 12 weeks, or you've received an IRS letter you don't understand. Abramson & Company can review the actual return behind the status and explain what's happening.
