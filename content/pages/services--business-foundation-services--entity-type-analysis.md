## Choosing the right structure shapes your tax and liability future

Every business decision starts with a structure. LLC, S-corp, C-corp, partnership, or sole proprietorship: each carries its own tax treatment, liability exposure, and paperwork burden. Pick wrong, and you could pay more in self-employment tax than necessary, or leave personal assets exposed to business debt. Pick right, and the structure quietly supports growth for years without you thinking about it again.

For business owners in Westport and across Fairfield County, entity type analysis is one of the first services under Abramson & Company's [Business Foundation Services](/services/business-foundation-services/). It's a deliberate review of income projections, liability risk, and long-term plans, conducted by a CPA who asks the right questions before recommending a direction. New ventures and established companies both need this review: the tax code doesn't stay still, and neither should your entity choice.

## Who benefits most from a professional entity review

New business owners face this decision on day one, often without knowing how much it matters. A [real estate](/industries/real-estate) investor forming an LLC for a rental property, a [professional service provider](/industries/professional-service-providers) setting up a consulting practice, a [retail](/industries/retail) owner opening a second location, or a video production company managing project-based revenue each face a different calculation. Guess wrong, and unwinding the paperwork later costs more than getting it right the first time.

Established businesses need this review too. A real estate professional adding a property management arm, a consulting firm bringing on partners, a retailer expanding into e-commerce, or a video production company transitioning from freelance to a formal business entity can all reach a point where the original structure no longer fits. Restructuring isn't a sign something went wrong. It's what growth looks like on paper.

<!-- block: process-steps | variant: vertical -->
## How our CPA-led process works

### Understanding your goals
The process starts with a conversation about where the business is headed: revenue targets, hiring plans, potential investors, and how you eventually want to exit. This context shapes everything that follows, more than any generic checklist could.

### Reviewing income and liability exposure
A CPA walks through projected income, self-employment tax exposure, and the personal liability risk tied to your current or planned structure. Real numbers replace guesswork at this stage.

### Weighing the options
LLC, S-corp, C-corp, partnership, or sole proprietorship: each option gets compared against your actual projections, not textbook assumptions. Some structures that look appealing on paper create tax problems once your specific numbers are run through them.

### Recommending a structure in plain language
You get a clear recommendation with the reasoning behind it, explained without jargon, so the decision makes sense before anything gets filed. Questions get answered on the spot, not weeks later in a follow-up email.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Entity type analysis

**Q: What is entity type analysis?**
A: Entity type analysis is a CPA review comparing how LLC, S-corp, C-corp, partnership, and sole proprietorship structures affect your taxes and personal liability. Abramson & Company evaluates your income projections and future plans before recommending which structure fits your specific business situation — whether you're in real estate, professional services, retail, or video production.

**Q: How do I know if my current business structure is still right?**
A: If your income has grown, you're adding partners, or you're facing new liability risk, your original structure may no longer fit. This is common for real estate investors expanding their portfolios, professional service firms adding partners, retailers opening new locations, and video production companies moving from freelance to a formal entity. A CPA review can compare your current setup against alternatives to see if restructuring would reduce taxes or better protect personal assets.

**Q: Does Abramson & Company help with entity setup outside Connecticut?**
A: Yes. Alongside Westport and Fairfield County, the firm serves clients across the tri-state area of Connecticut, New York, and New Jersey, plus clients in California's Bay Area, adjusting entity recommendations for state-specific rules.

**Q: What happens after my entity structure is chosen?**
A: Entity setup connects directly to bookkeeping, payroll, and tax services under Abramson & Company's fixed monthly fee model. A secure online platform keeps you and your CPA aligned on the numbers as the business moves forward.

**Q: What entity structure is best for a real estate investor?**
A: Most real estate investors benefit from LLC structures for liability protection, but the right choice depends on your portfolio size, whether you're flipping or holding, and how you're financing properties. Abramson & Company reviews your specific situation before making any recommendation.

**Q: What should video production companies consider when choosing a business entity?**
A: Video production companies often deal with project-based income, subcontractors, and equipment ownership — all of which affect the right entity choice. An S-corp or LLC can offer self-employment tax savings and liability protection that a sole proprietorship or informal partnership doesn't provide. A CPA review ensures the structure matches how your business actually operates.
