## LinkedIn

A client spent 200 hours last April coordinating a food drive at her Westport church, certain it should count as a tax deduction. It doesn't. The IRS will never let you deduct the value of your time or services, no matter your profession. But the money you spend out of pocket while volunteering, mileage, supplies, travel, is often deductible if you itemize and the organization is a qualified 501(c)(3).

The number that surprises most people: the charitable mileage rate is frozen at 14 cents per mile, set by Congress and unchanged for decades, versus the 67-cent business rate. Drive 40 miles round trip twice a week for Meals on Wheels in Fairfield County and that's roughly $580 a year in mileage alone. Any single expense of $250 or more needs written acknowledgment from the organization, not just a canceled check.
